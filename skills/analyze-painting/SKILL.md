# Analyze a painting

Method adapted from close looking in art history (describe, then interpret; Feldman's describe, analyze, interpret, judge). Written for a reader who is not an art historian: plain words first, the term in brackets.

## 1. Look before you write
- Open the image itself at full size, and crop details (brushwork, an edge, a face) when a claim rests on them. A contact sheet is not enough for brushwork.
- Note what the picture cannot show: a phone photo or a repost changes color and hides surface and scale; whether it is real paint, digital or AI may not be knowable (say which it looks like and why). Say so once.

## 2. Describe, element by element
One or two lines each, every claim something a reader can check in the picture (name the image number or the area: "lower left", "the seed heads"):
1. **Composition:** where the horizon or main mass sits, what leads the eye and in which direction, what is left empty.
2. **Value (light and dark):** how much of the picture is dark, where the lightest light is, hard or soft edges between them.
3. **Color:** the palette in plain names, how saturated, warm against cool, the one accent if there is one.
4. **Light:** its source and direction, hard or diffuse, what it picks out.
5. **Brushwork and surface (facture):** stroke length, direction, wet or dry, thick paint (impasto) or thin, loose near and tight far or the reverse, canvas texture.
6. **Space:** how depth is made (overlap, atmosphere, scale, value steps).

No adjective that only reports a feeling ("powerful", "stunning") unless the next words say what in the picture causes it.

## 3. Interpret
- **Mood, and what causes it:** tie each feeling to elements above ("calm because warm and cool differ only slightly").
- **Tradition:** the movement or painters it echoes, with the right period (Caravaggio is Baroque, about 1600, not Renaissance). Say "it echoes", not "it is", unless the artist is known.
- **Artist facts:** only what a source confirms; if the artist cannot be verified, say the post credits them and stop there.

## 4. The words to ask for it
3 to 6 terms or short phrases to search for more like it or to put in an image prompt (for example: chiaroscuro, tenebrism, Dutch flower still life, Russian mood landscape, alla prima, plein air), plus one line of prompt in English that would recreate the look without naming a living artist.

## 5. Tie it back
- If the user keeps a file of visual references, compare with one or two earlier saves of this kind: same principle or a different one.
- If one of their projects could use the technique, name it in one line.

## Output
Reply in this order: one-sentence answer (what makes it work), then sections 2 to 5, each short.
